# Golden Reaper - Pineapple, Lime & Carolina Reaper Mead (5L, 12%)

Tropical capsicumel made with Norwegian summer honey, pineapple, lime and one whole Carolina Reaper. This is deliberately an experimental, intensely chili-forward batch.

## Target Stats

* **Batch size:** ~5.0 L
* **Honey:** 1.71 kg
* **Expected OG:** ~1.105
* **Expected ABV if fermented dry:** 12%. ~14–15% before backsweetening
* **FG:** Ferment to completion; measure rather than target a fixed value
* **Final sweetness:** Adjust to taste after stabilization if necessary
* **Fermentation temperature:** 18–22°C
* **Chili level:** Intentionally very hot

# Carolina Reaper, Pineapple & Lime Purée

## Ingredients

* **250 g pineapple**
* **½ lime**
* **1 whole Carolina Reaper (~10 g)**
* **25–30 g brown sugar**

## Preparing the Reaper

Wear disposable gloves.

Remove the stem.

Keep the red flesh and internal placenta. The seeds may also remain.

Cut the pepper into several pieces before blending.

## Preparing the Lime

Use:

* the juice of **½ lime**
* finely grated green zest

Avoid adding substantial amounts of white pith, which can introduce bitterness.

## Blending

Blend together:

* **250 g pineapple**
* pineapple juice as required for blending
* **1 whole Carolina Reaper (~10 g)**
* juice and zest of **½ lime**

Blend thoroughly into a homogeneous purée.

## Pasteurisation

Transfer the purée to a small saucepan.

| Step | Temperature | Time | Action |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Heat | → 72–75°C | — | Heat gently while stirring |
| Pasteurise | **72–75°C** | **15 min** | Maintain temperature and stir occasionally |
| Cool | → 18–22°C | — | Keep covered while cooling |

Do **not boil**.

Once the purée has cooled substantially, stir in **25–30 g brown sugar** until dissolved.

The finished purée is added **unfiltered**, including all pineapple and Reaper solids.

### Carolina Reaper Safety

Blending and heating a whole Carolina Reaper can produce intensely irritating droplets and vapour.

* Wear gloves throughout preparation.
* Keep the room well ventilated.
* Do not put your face over the blender when opening it.
* Do not lean over the saucepan while heating.
* Avoid boiling or vigorous splashing.
* Thoroughly clean all equipment that contacted the pepper.

# Must Preparation

## Water Bill

| Addition | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Initial water | **3.0 L** |
| Top-up water | **1.5 L** |
| **Total water added** | **4.5 L** |

The final batch volume is approximately **5 L** after accounting for the dissolved honey and fruit purée.

## Procedure

1. Start with **3.0 L water**.
2. Warm enough of the water to approximately **50–60°C** to dissolve the honey easily.
3. Add **1.71 kg Sommerhonning** and stir until completely dissolved.
4. Do not boil the honey.
5. Add the cooled **250 g pineapple-lime-Carolina Reaper purée**, including all solids.
6. Add approximately **1.5 L additional water** as the top-up.
7. Mix extremely thoroughly.
8. Measure the OG.
9. Cool to pitching temperature if necessary.

Record the measured gravity rather than relying on the calculated OG:

# Fermentation

Cool the complete must to **18–22°C**.

Transfer to a sanitised fermenter with adequate headspace and aerate thoroughly.

Pitch **Mangrove Jack's M05 Mead Yeast**.

Because the batch contains finely blended pineapple solids, leave generous headspace during vigorous fermentation.

## Nutrient Schedule

Using DAP + ammonium-phosphate nutrient:

| Timing | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| At pitch | **2.0 g** |
| ~24 hours | **1.0 g** |
| ~48 hours | **1.0 g** |
| **Total** | **4.0 g** |

Dissolve each addition in a small amount of water or must before adding.

Gently swirl or degas before early nutrient additions. Be careful: pineapple solids can provide nucleation sites and cause substantial foaming.

## Reaper Extraction

Do **not** filter the purée during fermentation.

The pineapple and Reaper solids remain in contact with the mead, allowing extraction to continue as ethanol concentration increases.

This batch contains approximately **10 g of Carolina Reaper in only 5 L**, so the Reaper is intentionally a major component rather than a subtle background spice.

Taste periodically once primary fermentation begins slowing.

If the desired heat level is reached and fermentation is complete, there is no need to maintain prolonged contact with the solids simply to reach a predetermined number of days.

# Packaging

| Stage | Timing | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirm fermentation finished | SG stable for ≥3 days | Ensure fermentation is genuinely complete |
| Final chili assessment | Before cold crash | Evaluate heat before removing the solids |
| Cold crash | 48–72 h at 2–4°C | Compact yeast, pineapple and Reaper sediment |
| Rack | After cold crash | Leave fruit, pepper and yeast sediment behind |
| Backsweeten | After racking | Add honey to taste if required |
| Add K-meta | After backsweetening | Dissolve **0.30 g** and mix gently |
| Add potassium sorbate | After K-meta | Dissolve **0.8 g** separately and mix gently |
| Stabilization rest | 24–48 h at 2–4°C | Keep sealed and cold |
| Stability check | After stabilization | Verify gravity remains stable |
| Bottle | Once stable | Bottle still |
