# Hydromel à la Couque (5L, 12–13% ABV, fermented dry and backsweetened)

Hydromel au miel et couque épicée - Mead brewed with Belgian honey couque.

## Target Stats

* **Batch size**: 5.0 L
* **OG**: ~1.095–1.100
* **FG before backsweetening**: ~0.998–1.005
* **ABV**: ~12–13%
* **Final sweetness**: Adjust to taste after stabilization
* **Fermentation temperature**: 18–22°C

For a tighter 12–13% target, I would **not put the full 1.50 kg honey into primary**. Use about **1.35–1.40 kg**, and keep the remaining 100–150 g for backsweetening. The ~400 g bread already contributes roughly **172 g of sugar**.

## Water

### Profile (recommended)

Mead is fairly forgiving, so ordinary good-tasting brewing or drinking water is suitable.

* **Ca**: ~30–70 ppm
* **Mg**: ~5–15 ppm
* **Cl**: ~30–80 ppm
* **SO₄**: preferably below ~80 ppm
* Avoid strongly chlorinated water.

### PH

* Aim for approximately **pH 3.7–4.2 before fermentation**.
* Do not acidify aggressively before fermentation; mead pH often falls naturally once M05 becomes active.

## Recipe

### Mash Schedule

There is no true mash here because the bread's rye flour contains starch but you have no malt enzymes to convert it. The goal is simply to extract its **sugars, honey, cinnamon and bread character**.

| Minute | Step               | Target Temp (°C) | Action / Notes                                                  |
| ------ | ------------------ | ---------------: | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0      | Add bread to water |            20–30 | Tear ~400 g bread into small pieces and add to ~1.5–2.0 L water |
| 0–15   | Heat               |          20 → 72 | Stir gently while heating                                       |
| 15–35  | Extraction rest    |            70–75 | Hold for ~20 minutes; avoid boiling                             |
| 35     | Strain             |                — | Remove bread through a brewing bag or fine sieve                |
| 35–45  | Honey addition     |           ~50–65 | Dissolve 1.35–1.40 kg honey into the warm extract               |
| 45+    | Cool / dilute      |            18–22 | Add cold water to reach 5.0 L total                             |

#### Notes:

* Do **not** aggressively squeeze the bread pulp; that will push more rye starch into the must.
* Some haze is expected because of the flour/starch in the bread.
* Avoid boiling the honey to preserve more aroma.
* The bread already contains honey, pearl sugar, glucose-fructose syrup and cinnamon.

### Lauter / Sparge

* Let the bread drain naturally through the bag or sieve.
* A small rinse with **200–300 mL warm water** is fine if a lot of sugary liquid remains trapped.
* Include that rinse water when calculating the final **5.0 L** volume.
* After all honey has dissolved and the must is cool, top up precisely to 5.0 L.

Before pitching yeast, measure gravity if possible.

**Ideal OG: 1.095–1.100.**

If gravity is above about **1.105**, don't add more honey. If it is significantly below **1.090**, you can dissolve a little of the reserved honey into the must.

### Cooling

* Cool the must to **18–22°C**.
* Transfer into the sanitised **6 L fermenter**.
* Keep total volume at approximately **5.0 L**.
* Aerate thoroughly before pitching yeast.

### Fermentation

**Inorganic nitrogen nutrient: diammonium phosphate (DAP) + ammonium phosphate**
* At pitch: 2.0 g
* ~24 hours: 1.0 g
* ~48 hours: 1.0 g

Gently swirl or degas before early nutrient additions. Dissolve each addition in a small amount of water or must before adding.

### Fermentation Timeline

| Day    | Stage                |   Approx. SG | Notes                                                               |
| ------ | -------------------- | -----------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0      | Pitch M05            |  1.095–1.100 | Aerate well; first nutrient addition                                |
| 1      | Active fermentation  | ~1.070–1.085 | Second nutrient addition                                            |
| 2      | Strong fermentation  | ~1.050–1.070 | Final nutrient addition                                             |
| 3–7    | Main fermentation    |      Falling | Leave undisturbed once early nutrient additions are finished        |
| 7–14   | Slowing fermentation | ~1.005–1.020 | Bread sediment will begin settling                                  |
| 14–21+ | Finishing            | ~0.998–1.005 | Check gravity                                                       |
| 21+    | Stable FG            | ~0.998–1.005 | Gravity must remain unchanged for several days before stabilization |

### Packaging

| Stage                         | Timing                           | What to do                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Confirm fermentation finished | SG stable for at least 3 days    | Make sure primary fermentation is genuinely complete.             |
| Cold crash                    | 48–72 h at 2–4°C                 | Compact yeast and bread sediment.                                 |
| Rack                          | Immediately after cold crash     | Siphon into a clean, sanitized vessel, leaving sediment behind.   |
| Backsweeten                   | Immediately after racking        | Add honey to taste and mix gently but thoroughly.                 |
| Add K-meta                    | Immediately after backsweetening | Dissolve **0.30 g** in a small amount of water and mix in gently. |
| Add potassium sorbate         | Immediately after K-meta         | Dissolve **0.8 g** separately and mix in gently.                  |
| Stabilization rest            | 24–48 h at 2–4°C                 | Keep sealed and cold.                                             |
| Stability check               | After stabilization              | Confirm gravity is not dropping.                                  |
| Bottle                        | Once stable                      | Bottle still.                                                     |

For backsweetening, don't automatically dump in a fixed quantity. Start with the honey you reserved from primary.

For this mead, I'd expect something around **150–250 g backsweetening honey** to be an interesting range, but let the finished dry mead decide.

A useful bench trial:

* Take **100 mL mead**.
* Add **2 g honey**, mix and taste.
* If necessary, try another sample at **3 g**, **4 g**, etc. per 100 mL.

That corresponds to:

| Honey in 100 mL sample | Equivalent for 5 L |
| ---------------------: | -----------------: |
|                    2 g |              100 g |
|                    3 g |              150 g |
|                    4 g |              200 g |
|                    5 g |              250 g |
|                    6 g |              300 g |

### Conditioning Time – Overview

| Method       | Conditioning Time  | Notes                                                                   |
| ------------ | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bulk aging   | 1–3 months         | Recommended; bread, cinnamon and alcohol will integrate                 |
| Bottled      | 2–3 months minimum | Drinkable earlier, but likely substantially smoother with age           |
| Longer aging | 6+ months          | Should soften the alcohol and integrate the rye/bread character further |

---

## Final Notes

* **Use 1.35–1.40 kg of your 1.50 kg honey in primary**, rather than all of it. With the bread's ~172 g sugar contribution, this gives you much better control around your **12–13% target**.
* Keep the remaining honey for **backsweetening**.
* Expect a **large sediment layer** from the rye flour, yeast and bread particles.
* The finished dry mead may initially taste more alcoholic and less honey-like than expected. The fresh honey character will return strongly when you backsweeten.
* Since the bread already contains **cinnamon**, skip the mjødurt.
